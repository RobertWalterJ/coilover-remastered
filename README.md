# Coilover Remastered

A low poly off roader with seventy centimetres of suspension you can watch
working. Personal project, not GPA.

**This is the remaster.** The original is at
[RobertWalterJ/coilover](https://github.com/RobertWalterJ/coilover) and still
runs as a single file with every model generated in code. This fork loads a
Blender asset pack instead: modelled vehicles, props, flora, roads and
bridges, authored to the same specification the game was already using. The
hand built shells are still in `src/game.html` and are used as the fallback
whenever a model is missing, so the game degrades to the original rather than
breaking.

Double-click **Launch Coilover.bat**. It starts a local server and opens the
game. The window it opens also prints an address like `http://192.168.x.x:8797`
which you can open on your phone over the same Wi-Fi, then use *Add to Home
Screen*. After the first load it runs with no signal.

There is also a private Artifact version, which is the easiest way to play on
the phone without leaving the laptop running.

MIT licensed, and there is nothing in here that is not mine except three.js —
see [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md). Every texture, sound,
vehicle and landscape in the game is generated by the code at load, which is
why the whole thing is one HTML file with no assets beside it.

## Controls

The lower half of the screen is two invisible zones. Left steers, right drives.

| | |
|---|---|
| Left half, lower | Slide your thumb to steer. Wherever it lands is straight ahead. An arc appears under it while you hold. |
| Gas ring | Throttle. In the air it lifts the nose. |
| Brake ring | Reads the situation. Above 25 km/h it drops rear grip and the back steps out. Below that it is a brake. Held at a standstill it reverses. |
| Double tap the sky | Change camera |
| Dot, top right | Pause, garage, settings, map |
| Keyboard | WASD or arrows, space for handbrake, R to upright, C for camera |

Four pedal layouts in Settings. **Two** is the default pair of rings. **One**
puts throttle and brake on a single pad: press and slide up for more throttle,
below the dead band for ever more brake, with the fill running downward for
braking because that is the direction the force is. **One handed** adds
steering to the same thumb on the x axis. **Arcade** is full throttle or full
brake, nothing in between.

Across the top: score on the left, speed in the middle, menu on the right.

## What is in it

**Three places to drive.** Each is 560 metres square and none of them is
another one repainted.

*Ochre Basin* is the desert: dunes ringed by a wall you can see rather than an
invisible barrier, a dry lakebed that is dead flat and fast, a whoop section,
three launch ramps, a rock ridge and a mesa. Twelve gates stand in a loop
around it and they are the circuit.

*Vantage Hill* is a city on hills with a flat pan on the water side for long
drifts. The buildings are placed models carrying their own collision, not mass
stitched into the terrain, and the streets measure three times smoother than
the flattest desert surface.

*Pike Narrows* is Canadian Shield, north of cottage country. Rolling granite
whalebacks the ice rode smooth, five lakes all at one water table, muskeg in
the hollows that will bog you down, an esker to run along, and a logging road
that crosses the water on causeways and timber bridges. Camps on the shore with
lit windows and a canoe on the dock, a general store with gas pumps, a fire
tower on the highest dome.

**Five vehicles.** A utility 4x4, a Dakar rally raid truck, a lifted rear
engine coupe, a lifted mid engine wedge, and a downforce car that makes more
than its own weight in aero flat out. Each is a different set of springs,
tyres, drive split, gearing and body, and they are earned rather than handed
over.

**Eight places worth finding.** A gas station, a motel sign, a drive in screen,
a water tower, a half buried aircraft, an abandoned camp, a windmill and a ring
of stones. Quiet in daylight, lit at dusk and night. Reach one and a flag goes
up and it appears on your map.

**Five hours of the day.** Dawn, Morning, Golden, Dusk, Night. Each is a
complete lighting contract covering sun angle and colour, fill, all five sky
gradient stops, fog and how much anything glows. They cross fade. There is an
optional slow drift, off by default.

**Things that count up.** An odometer that never resets. A map that paints
itself in from where the wheels have actually been this session. And at each
ramp, a stack of stones marking your longest clean landing, which physically
relocates to the new spot while you are still driving away from it.

**A session, and a career.** Two different questions, so two different numbers.
The corner counts what you have scored since you opened the game and starts at
zero every time. The career total never resets: it buys the vehicles and the
third map, and it sets a rank, ten of them from Green to Immortal.

**Three open runs at a time.** Short, concrete, and replaced the moment one is
done — hold a speed, land a jump of a stated size, reach a chain, hold a drift
of a given length, find a landmark, beat one of your own segment records. They
are what the driving is *for*, and they are drawn from whichever map you are
on.

**Segments and the circuit.** Named stretches of each map keep your best score
through them, Strava fashion. On the desert the twelve gates are a lap: the
first one starts a clock, each next gate has a window to be reached in, the
gates pay more the deeper into the lap you are, and finishing records a time
worth going back to beat.

No grading, no percentage complete, no results screen, and nothing that shows
you what you have not found. The only clock in the game counts up, and only
while you are on a circuit lap.

## The physics

Four independent struts, each a real spring and damper. The strut casts down
the body's own down axis, solves against the local ground plane, and applies
its force at the contact patch, so roll and pitch fall out rather than being
scripted. Progressive bump stops give back fifty five percent of what they
absorb, which is why landing on a downslope relaunches you.

Tyres use a simplified magic formula with a slow post peak falloff, so at full
lock sideways there is still sixty five percent of peak grip available. That
slow decay is what makes a slide somewhere you can live rather than a cliff.
Slip angle is filtered by distance travelled, not by time, which removes
terrain jitter and adds the small lag that makes counter steering feel like a
conversation. Load sensitivity is one line and buys lift off oversteer and
trail braking for free.

Three deliberate choices that look wrong and are not:

- **Force acts at the contact patch, but the torque arm uses a shortened
  virtual height**, 0.68 m for lateral and 1.05 m for longitudinal. Using the
  true arm puts the effective centre of gravity 1.14 m up and the truck tips
  from cornering alone. Using a short arm for both loses the dive and squat.
- **Air control targets an attitude, not a rotation rate.** Rate control means
  holding the gas winds the body up without limit. Measured: three somersaults
  in a two and a half second jump, landing attitude 0.16 upright. With a PD
  controller onto a target attitude it lands at 0.87.
- **A landing is judged against the terrain normal, not world level.** Nose
  down onto a downslope is a good landing and world level cannot tell.

## Layout

```
src/game.html        THE SOURCE. Edit this, and nothing else.
src/patches/         the one-off scripts that built it, kept for the reasoning
                     in their docstrings. See the README in there.
build.py             src/game.html -> docs/index.html. Run after every edit.
docs/                the built game. ONE output, not two: this is both what
                     GitHub Pages publishes and what server.mjs serves, so an
                     asset cannot exist twice at two versions.
  index.html         generated; do not edit by hand
  assets/            the Blender pack: vehicles, props, flora, roads, bridges
  vendor/            three.js r128 and GLTFLoader, vendored to run offline
  sw.js              service worker; caches every model so an install works
                     with no signal
server.mjs           dependency free static server on port 8797
ASSETS.md            the asset pack's own integration notes
ASSET-AUDIT.md       the audit of the original game the pack was built against
```

`src/game.html` is exactly what publishes as the Artifact. The only difference
in the built copy is that three.js comes from `vendor/` instead of the CDN,
plus the PWA head tags and the service worker registration.

## Notes to self

- Add `?debug` for `window.coilover`. It exposes the rig, the landmarks, the
  save data, and `tick(seconds)`, which runs the simulation headless and
  renders a frame. Use `tick` for any test: a backgrounded tab throttles
  `requestAnimationFrame` to nothing and every timing based test silently
  returns zeros.
- **When a feature does nothing in a headless test, suspect the harness before
  the feature.** `tick` has to run every layer the real loop runs. Four
  separate things looked broken — the frame rate dependent layer, the clock,
  the speedometer, the counting tally — purely because `tick` was not calling
  the function they live in. It now runs `sync`, `updateLightPool`,
  `updateCam`, `revs`, `hud`, `popStep` and `audioTick`.
- **Average any terrain test over five or more start points.** A single run
  diverges, so two configurations never drive over the same ground and the
  comparison is noise. Three rounds were spent tuning the wrong parameter
  before this was obvious. Keep start points away from the basin rim: driving
  up the wall and launching off it looks exactly like a suspension fault.
- **`MeshLambertMaterial` ignores `flatShading` in r128.** Lambert lights per
  vertex, so there is no fragment normal to facet. Everything is
  `MeshPhongMaterial` with `shininess: 0` and black specular, which is Lambert
  evaluated per fragment. This was silently flattening the entire look.
- **Colour space.** Material and light colours feed linear lighting maths and
  must go through `convertSRGBToLinear()`. Fog and the sky shader composite in
  display space and must not. Getting this backwards washes the palette out.
- The terrain grid is jittered in x and z before sampling height, and each
  quad picks the diagonal with the smaller height difference. Without those two
  the heightmap shows a herringbone grain from any angle.
- Physics reads the analytic height function, never the mesh, so the jitter
  never touches collision.
- The service worker is network first for the page. If the phone ever sticks on
  an old build, bump `CACHE` in `app/sw.js`.
